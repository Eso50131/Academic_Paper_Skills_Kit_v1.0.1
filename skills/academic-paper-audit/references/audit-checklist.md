# Independent Academic Paper Audit Checklist

Use this as a coverage checklist, not a mechanical scoring rubric. Mark each item `Pass / Issue / Not verifiable / Not applicable` and cite the location/evidence.

## 1. Research factual integrity

- [ ] Research object, scope, and conditions match the original materials.
- [ ] Every described method/model/algorithm was actually used or is clearly labelled as proposed.
- [ ] Code architecture, input/output, loss functions, assumptions, and parameters match inspected implementation where available.
- [ ] Dataset/source, sample count, inclusion rules, and preprocessing match records.
- [ ] Experimental/simulation setup, boundary conditions, loads, controls, and environment match records where relevant.
- [ ] Numerical values, units, denominators, thresholds, and calculations are traceable.
- [ ] No plan, intention, or expected result is presented as completed work.
- [ ] No fabricated result, comparison, ablation, test, approval, or validation is present.
- [ ] Contradictions between paper, Dossier, code, data, and logs are reported.

## 2. Literature and citation integrity

- [ ] Every cited source exists.
- [ ] Author, title, year, venue, and DOI/URL metadata are accurate where checked.
- [ ] The cited source supports the specific attached claim.
- [ ] Attribution is correct; one paper's finding is not assigned to another.
- [ ] Abstract-only inspection is not represented as full-text review.
- [ ] Direct quotations are exact and traceable.
- [ ] Important foundational or directly relevant work is not obviously omitted.
- [ ] Novelty and “first/no prior work” claims have an adequate search basis.
- [ ] Current standards and target-venue instructions use the correct version.
- [ ] Review-paper source selection and synthesis are transparent.

## 3. Plan compliance

- [ ] The manuscript follows the approved paper genre and scope.
- [ ] The central research question is preserved.
- [ ] Approved core claims are included or omissions are explained.
- [ ] No unapproved method, experiment, result, or contribution has been added as fact.
- [ ] Material user corrections are reflected.
- [ ] Important unresolved items in the Dossier are not silently presented as resolved.
- [ ] Any substantive change after outline approval is recorded and justified.
- [ ] Plan compliance is marked not assessed if the Dossier is absent.

## 4. Argumentation and logical validity

- [ ] The problem follows logically from the context and literature.
- [ ] The research gap is specific and supported.
- [ ] Research objectives are answerable by the stated method and evidence.
- [ ] The method addresses the actual research problem.
- [ ] Assumptions are stated and reasonable within the scope.
- [ ] Experimental design can answer the stated question.
- [ ] Baselines and comparisons are fair and sufficiently described.
- [ ] Results support the interpretation.
- [ ] Discussion adds interpretation rather than merely repeating results.
- [ ] No causal claim exceeds the design.
- [ ] No unsupported inference or logical leap remains.

## 5. Contribution and novelty validity

- [ ] Contributions are specific, not merely adjectives.
- [ ] Methodological, theoretical, empirical, and engineering contributions are distinguished.
- [ ] Contribution claims are compared with relevant prior work.
- [ ] “First”, “novel”, “unprecedented”, and “fills a gap” are properly qualified.
- [ ] Implementation work is not mislabeled as theoretical novelty.
- [ ] Claimed generality matches the validation range.

## 6. Results and conclusion consistency

- [ ] Results are reported completely enough to support the main claims.
- [ ] Negative, null, mixed, or unexpected results are not hidden when material.
- [ ] Statistical claims have the required test, assumptions, and uncertainty information.
- [ ] “Significant” is not ambiguous between statistical and colloquial meaning.
- [ ] Conclusions answer the research question.
- [ ] Conclusions do not introduce new findings.
- [ ] Conclusions do not exceed the tested conditions.
- [ ] Limitations are specific and meaningful.
- [ ] Future work is not described as current capability.

## 7. Genre, structure, and venue

- [ ] The manuscript's genre is correctly identified.
- [ ] The structure fits the genre and discipline.
- [ ] A review synthesizes rather than merely lists papers.
- [ ] A systematic/scoping review reports the actual search and screening process.
- [ ] A meta-analysis reports appropriate quantitative synthesis.
- [ ] A methods paper contains sufficient technical specification and validation.
- [ ] The abstract accurately reflects the body.
- [ ] Section functions are distinct and appropriate.
- [ ] Venue-specific requirements were checked if a venue was named.
- [ ] Generic formatting is not falsely described as venue-compliant.

## 8. Academic expression and anti-AI quality

- [ ] The introduction reaches the specific problem without excessive generic background.
- [ ] Paragraphs have clear central functions.
- [ ] Sentences maintain information continuity.
- [ ] Contrastive constructions are used only for meaningful distinctions.
- [ ] Transitions express real logical relations.
- [ ] Empty praise and unsupported impact language are removed.
- [ ] Internal development history and irrelevant rejected options are omitted.
- [ ] Technical terms are stable and precise.
- [ ] Repetition across abstract, introduction, results, discussion, and conclusion is controlled.
- [ ] Language is clear rather than artificially ornate.
- [ ] The paper does not make every result sound positive.
- [ ] Approved, evidence-supported core claims retain their justified strength; no material weakening or withdrawal is left unexplained.
- [ ] New hedges, caveats, disclaimers, and scope restrictions are supported by specific evidence or a material reader need.
- [ ] Genuine limitations and uncertainty are retained, while repetitive or disproportionate disclaimers are avoided.
- [ ] Hypothetical reviewer objections have not been inserted automatically into the manuscript.
- [ ] If a baseline is available, material claim changes are compared and recorded; if not, no unsupported inference is made about prior revisions.

## 9. Research transparency and visual integrity

- [ ] Methods are reproducible to the extent expected for the genre.
- [ ] Data/material availability statements are accurate.
- [ ] Ethics, consent, funding, conflict, and author-contribution statements are accurate where required.
- [ ] Figures and tables are numbered consistently and cited in text.
- [ ] Captions, notes, units, axes, legends, and abbreviations are clear.
- [ ] Underlying figure/table data are checked where supplied.
- [ ] Markdown conversion has not dropped figures, tables, equations, captions, or supplementary references.
- [ ] Image links resolve or missing assets are explicitly marked.
- [ ] Mathematical symbols and equations are consistent.
- [ ] AI-use disclosure follows the target venue's current policy.
